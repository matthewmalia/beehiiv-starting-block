# Your welcome email: three prompts

Your welcome email is the first thing every new reader gets from you. **You write it.** Answer these three prompts in your own words, in order, and what you have is the email. Aim for 100–200 words in total. Save your answers to `inbox/welcome.md` or paste them into the chat, and the editor prepares it like any post (in `posts/_welcome/`).

## 1. What will readers get, and when will it arrive?

Your promise and your send day. One or two sentences.

> Example shape (don't copy it): "Every Tuesday morning you'll get one …"

## 2. Why do you write this?

Why this subject, and why you. For a brand: what your company believes about this topic. Two to four sentences. This is where a new reader decides whether to open the next one.

## 3. What's one question you'd like them to answer by reply?

Ask one easy, specific question and tell them you read the answers. Replies help your emails land in the inbox, and they fill your ideas list.

> Example shape: "What's the one thing about … you're stuck on right now? Hit reply. I read every one."

---

## Also decide

- **Subject line** (about 50 characters or fewer). Plain beats clever here: readers should recognise it as the thing they just signed up for.
- **Preview text**: one line that continues the subject, not a repeat of it.
- **Sign-off**: the same one you'll use on every post.

## What happens next

1. The editor fixes mechanical errors, sends you any suggestions, and prepares the final text.
2. The Publishing Lead creates the automation as a draft (`templates/welcome-automation.json`), and the editor places your text in it.
3. **You** open the automation in the beehiiv app, read it once more, publish it and turn it on. Nothing reaches a subscriber until you do.

## Later: a three-email series

Once the single welcome email is live and you've written two more emails, the same flow extends to: signup → email 1 → wait 2 days → email 2 → wait 3 days → email 3 (only for readers who opened something). Automations need a paid plan after the trial; on Free, keep the single welcome email.
